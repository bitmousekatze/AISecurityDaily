Dark Humor Nuclear Scale Rating · Weekend Edition · July 16 – 18, 2026
9.4
Level "The Approval Dialog Was Cosplay"

Last week your tools stole from you. This week they gaslit you.

You clicked "Approve." The write went somewhere else. You asked your AI what to install. It invented a package, and a stranger had already built the malware version, because they asked your AI first. Your agent's memory? Poisoned nine sessions ago. It remembers things that never happened, on purpose, for someone else's benefit.

Security theater used to require a theater. Now the popup itself is the performance.

  • 👻
    GhostApproval — Wiz tested six AI coding assistants to see if the permission dialog actually guards the thing it claims to guard. Six for six: nope. A symlink in a cloned repo and your "write to README.md" approval lands in ~/.bashrc. Half the vendors patched. The other half said this is "within the threat model," which is corporate for "we drew the threat model around the bug." The human-in-the-loop was in the loop. The loop was a noose.
  • 🎵
    Suno — An npm worm from LAST NOVEMBER ate an employee laptop, and eight months later the entire training-data pipeline is on the internet: 113,000 hours of YouTube Music, comments in the code politely documenting how to dodge the scraping protections. The labels suing them just got handed discovery on a silver platter by a hacker named ellie.191. Suno's response: it's "outdated source code." Ah yes, the crimes were legacy crimes.
  • 🧠
    HalluSquatting — Attackers stopped waiting for your typos. They just ask the same AI you ask, note which packages it hallucinates, register those names, and stuff them with botnet droppers. Your assistant recommends the malware BY NAME, confidently, with example code. It's the first supply chain attack where the vendor of the vulnerability is your own autocomplete.
  • 💉
    The Injection Scoreboard — Mid-year numbers dropped: tool-input injection works 84% of the time. Memory poisoning survives 9 sessions. Prompt injection up 340% year-over-year, OWASP #1 for the fourth straight edition. At some point we have to stop calling it a vulnerability and start calling it a feature of the architecture. The model cannot tell instructions from data. That's not a bug report; that's a diagnosis.
  • 🏛️
    The Clearinghouse — The White House launched a coordination group to share vulnerabilities that AI systems discover. Same day: a dairy company halted milk production because of hackers, and Abbott is running two simultaneous breach investigations like it's a twin-stick shooter. The government is building a group chat. The attackers are building botnets out of hallucinations. Godspeed, everyone.

"The approval dialog asked if I was sure. It never asked if IT was sure."

July 15–16, 2026 · The Receipts Drop
Suno's Training Data Goes Public
  • ellie.191 publishes the goods: Source code with internal comments cheerfully cataloguing 113K hours of YouTube Music, 62K of Pond5, 17K of Genius, 12K of Deezer, and a to-do list targeting 420,000 podcasts. The most organized data heist documentation since someone put "steal_youtube.py" in a public repo.
  • Entry vector: the Shai-Hulud worm. Yes, that one. The May npm worm. It never left. It was just waiting in someone's node_modules like a landmine with a changelog.
Breach in November. Disclosure in July. The notification timeline was "when the hacker felt like it." Incident response by press cycle.
July 16, 2026 · Autocomplete Becomes a Threat Actor
HalluSquatting Farms Your AI's Lies
  • The workflow: ask the model for code → note the packages it invents → register them → add botnet dropper → wait. The victims install it because their AI told them to. With confidence. In a code block. Possibly with a little sparkle emoji.
  • Bonus round — Friendly Fire: agents tricked into running attacker code during security scans. The scan is the payload. The antivirus is the virus. We are through the looking glass.
Typosquatting needed you to fail. HalluSquatting only needs the model to be confidently wrong — a renewable resource. Infinite malware, zero user error required.
July 17, 2026 · Coordination Nation
White House Builds A Group Chat
  • AI vulnerability clearinghouse launches: AI developers and critical-infrastructure operators will now share the bugs their AIs find. A genuinely reasonable idea, delivered at the speed of government, into a battlefield moving at the speed of inference.
  • Meanwhile, in reality: Fairlife halts milk production (ransomware got the dairy), Abbott juggles two breaches at once, and the mid-year injection stats read like a eulogy. The milk is offline. The MILK.
The framework has no enforcement, no penalties, no deadlines. It's a LinkedIn post with a seal on it. The attackers were not available for comment; they were busy.
July 18, 2026 · The Dialog Confesses
GhostApproval: Six Assistants, Zero Boundaries
  • The symlink trick lands on everyone: Amazon Q, Claude Code, Augment, Cursor, Antigravity, Windsurf. The dialog says "write to notes.txt." The filesystem says "write to your shell profile." You said yes. Technically, consent was obtained.
  • Vendor bingo: three patch it, others file it under "working as intended." The threat model, it turns out, is load-bearing paperwork.
The entire agentic safety story was "a human approves every write." The human approved. The write lied. Human-in-the-loop, filesystem-out-of-the-loop.

Your AI hallucinated a package, an attacker made it real, your other AI approved installing it, and the write landed in your shell profile. At no point did a human make an unassisted decision. This is called productivity.

— The weekend, summarized for the incident report

Here's the uncomfortable arc of July so far. Week one: the tools phone home. Week two: the tools upload your home. This weekend: the tools lie about what they're doing while they do it.

The approval prompt approves something else. The privacy policy meant nothing eight months before you read it. The package recommendation is attacker-supplied via your model's imagination. The agent's memory contains someone else's instructions, planted last Tuesday, still steering.

You cannot review your way out of this with vibes. The boundary has to be structural: sandboxes, lockfiles, least privilege, out-of-band confirmation. Anything the model touches is untrusted. Including the parts that look like you wrote them.

And rotate your credentials. Not because of a specific incident. Just — gesture broadly — because.
