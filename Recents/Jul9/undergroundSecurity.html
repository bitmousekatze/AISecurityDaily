Level "The Call Is Coming From Inside The Editor"

Last week your editor believed strangers.

This week two superpowers got into a public argument about whose surveillance is in your editor, and the honest answer was yes.

China's national vulnerability database — a government body — formally warned the world that Claude Code contained a backdoor shipping your region and identity to remote servers. Anthropic's rebuttal, and we are summarizing faithfully: it wasn't a backdoor, it was an experiment; the experiment is being removed; and also the affected users weren't supposed to be customers in the first place. Somewhere a philosophy department is fighting over whether code can be a vulnerability if the victim was unauthorized to be victimized.

  • 🚪
    Claude Code "backdoor" — versions 2.1.91 through 2.1.196 quietly checked your timezone and hostname against a list of Chinese company domains and phoned the results home. Beijing calls it a backdoor. Anthropic calls it anti-distillation telemetry. Your firewall calls it Tuesday. One hundred and six versions. That's not a leak, that's a release cadence. Issue #13 →
  • 🖥️
    Hijacked Ollama servers are now wired into automated scan-exploit-breach pipelines. Attackers took the exposed AI boxes we warned about in Issue #6, and instead of stealing tokens, they built the thing every CISO has wanted for a decade: a fully automated penetration testing program. It's just pointed the wrong way. And running on your GPU. Issue #13 →
  • 🐳
    Gitea's official Docker image shipped with REVERSE_PROXY_TRUSTED_PROXIES = * — the asterisk that trusts the entire internet to tell it who's logged in. Send a header, become the admin. CVE-2026-20896 · CVSS 9.8. Now being probed in the wild. Issue #13 →
  • 🎆
    Adobe's July 1 patch drop — seven perfect CVSS 10.0s — got exploited within hours of disclosure. Adobe's response: move to twice-monthly security bulletins, because AI is finding bugs faster than the quarterly-PDF culture can absorb. The machines are speedrunning both sides of this now.
  • 🧠
    "CoT Forgery" research: attackers inject fabricated chain-of-thought into prompts and the model trusts it, because it looks like its own thinking. Sixty percent success against frontier models. The models have officially inherited our most human vulnerability: believing anything phrased in our own inner voice.

On the nuclear dark-humor scale we've reached "the geopolitical dispute is about which country gets to read your hostname."

For twelve issues the threat came from what your tools read — a config, a comment, a search result. This week it came from what your tools are. The agent binary had opinions. The Docker default had trust issues. The security product had a side hustle.

And the actual defensive advice, stripped of all flags and press releases, is identical no matter who issues it: know what leaves your machine. When your newsletter and a ministry of state security are giving the same tip, it's probably load-bearing.

Update Claude Code. Patch Gitea. Close the Ollama port. And log off on time — the packets will still be captured tomorrow.
