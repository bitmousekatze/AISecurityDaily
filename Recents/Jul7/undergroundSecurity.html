Dark Humor Nuclear Scale Rating · The Week of June 30 – July 7, 2026
9.8
Level "Zero-Click — You Didn't Even Do Anything"

Last week we joked that "open a repo" had become a code-execution decision.

This week the industry replied: hold my sandbox.

Cursor disclosed two zero-click prompt injections — CVSS 9.8, both — where the attack is that your agent reads something. A poisoned MCP response. A web search result. That's it. That's the whole exploit. And the best part, the part that belongs in a museum: when researchers reported it in February, Cursor rejected the report in four days, because MCP misuse "does not fit the threat model." The threat model has since been updated. By the threat.

  • 🏖️
    Cursor "DuneSlide" — two zero-click prompt injections escape the sandbox and hand over your whole machine. CVE-2026-50548 · CVE-2026-50549 · CVSS 9.8 ×2. Reported in February. Rejected in four days: not in the threat model. Narrator: it was in the threat model. Issue #12 →
  • 🧰
    JetBrains patched a critical cluster across basically everything with its logo on it. GoLand ran attacker code when you opened a project. Hub generated account-recovery codes with a weak RNG, so anyone could recover your account, including admin. CVE-2026-56141 · CVSS 9.8 Issue #12 →
  • 🎣
    North Korea went multi-registry — 108 malicious packages across npm, Packagist, Go modules, and one Chrome extension for the completionists. Delivery mechanism: a fake recruiter and a take-home assignment. The interview process is now an exploit chain, which honestly tracks. Issue #12 →
  • 🤖
    "JadePuffer" ransomware deployed an AI agent that automates its entire attack lifecycle, initial access through encryption. Attackers now have agentic workflows too. Somewhere out there, a ransomware operator is arguing with his agent about hallucinated victim hostnames, and that thought is the only thing keeping us going.
  • ☢
    And CISA's KEV catalog kept the streak alive — SharePoint's deserialization RCE landed with a July 4 deadline (happy Independence Day, patch faster), while last week's SimpleHelp entry sailed past its own deadline, still actively exploited, four days overdue.

☢ The Complete Week of Doom — Day by Day
February 19–23, 2026 · The Prequel Issue #12 →
Researchers Report Two 9.8s. Vendor Says "No Thanks."
  • Cato AI Labs hands Cursor two zero-click prompt-injection-to-RCE bugs on February 19. On February 23 the report is rejected — MCP server misuse "does not fit the threat model," even via standard, innocuous integrations.
  • Both bugs get quietly fixed in Cursor 3.0 anyway (April 2). The CVEs and the public writeup arrive this week, wearing an "I told you so" that can be seen from orbit.
"Not in our threat model" is a load-bearing sentence. The threat, unfortunately, does not read the threat model. It writes it.
July 1, 2026 · JetBrains Patch Cluster Issue #12 →
Every JetBrains Logo Gets a CVE
  • Hub generated account-restore codes with a weak RNG CVE-2026-56141CVSS 9.8 — unauthenticated attackers could enumerate codes and take over any account, admin included. Plus an auth bypass CVE-2026-50242 and a privilege-escalation bug CVE-2026-56142 for the collectors.
  • GoLand executed attacker-controlled project config on open. Kotlin ran arbitrary code from poisoned build-cache metadata. IntelliJ had command injection via filename completion. The autocomplete. The thing you use to avoid typing.
The IDE has the source, the SSH keys, the API tokens, and the deploy pipeline. It is the softest, warmest target in the building, and it just told you it trusts whatever's in the project folder.
July 2, 2026 · DuneSlide Goes Public Issue #12 →
Zero Clicks. Two CVEs. One Sandbox, Purely Decorative.
  • Cursor "DuneSlide" hits the news — CVE-2026-50548 CVE-2026-50549CVSS 9.8 ×2. One flaw lets a poisoned MCP response or web search result steer the terminal tool's working_directory anywhere on disk; the other walks out through a symlink. Full OS-level RCE, no user interaction.
  • Attack surface: anything the agent reads. Last week the malicious input was a config file. This week it's a search result. Next week it's presumably a vibe.
The sandbox was a suggestion. The agent was the escape hatch. You didn't click anything — that's the feature and the vulnerability.
July 4, 2026 · KEV Deadline Day
SharePoint's Patch Deadline Lands on a Federal Holiday
  • Microsoft SharePoint deserialization RCE CVE-2026-45659CVSS 8.8 — actively exploited, federal patch deadline July 4. Microsoft fixed it in May and just… didn't mention it existed for weeks. Surprise patches for a surprise party.
  • Meanwhile last week's SimpleHelp auth bypass CVE-2026-48558 blew through its July 2 deadline, still actively exploited, TaskWeaver still dropping. And Adobe ColdFusion shipped 11 criticals — six rated a perfect 10.0 — into its historical average of being exploited within days.
Assigning a KEV deadline of July 4th is the most honest thing CISA has ever done: the fireworks were always going to happen either way.
July 6, 2026 · PolinRider Mapped Issue #12 →
The Job Interview Is the Malware Now
  • North Korea's PolinRider campaign gets fully mapped: 108 malicious packages, 162 artifacts — 19 npm, 10 Packagist, 61 Go modules, and one Chrome extension, because apparently the DPRK believes in full-stack coverage more than most startups.
  • Delivery: the "Contagious Interview" playbook. A friendly recruiter, an exciting role, a take-home task, and a repo that takes you home instead. Also spotted this week: the JadePuffer ransomware crew running an AI agent that automates the entire attack lifecycle. Everyone's shipping agents. Everyone.
The take-home assignment now comes with its own supply chain. If a recruiter's coding task insists on running on your main machine tonight, the job is real — it's just not your job. It's theirs.

Every major way a developer opens code — the AI editor, the classic IDE, the package manager, the job interview — shipped an "opening code runs code" bug within fourteen days. The file browser is now a deployment target.

— The threat model, writing itself again

On the nuclear dark-humor scale we've reached "the vulnerability report was rejected because being vulnerable wasn't in the plan."

Editors that believe strangers' prompts. IDEs that trust whatever's in the folder. Recruiters whose take-home tasks take your tokens home. And ransomware crews with their own agentic workflows, living the same automation dream we are.

None of it is new. That's the punchline. It's the same story as Issue #11, except this time nobody even had to open a config file. Reading was enough.

Update Cursor. Update JetBrains. Interview inside a VM. And log off on time — the asteroid will still be uncomfortably shaped tomorrow.
